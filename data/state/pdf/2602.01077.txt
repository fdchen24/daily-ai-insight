Abstract
Diffusion Transformers are fundamental for visual generation, but their efficiency
is bottlenecked by the quadratic complexity of self-attention. While block-sparse
attention accelerates computation by attending only to critical key-value blocks,
it suffers from quality degradation at high sparsity due to discarding substantial
context. In this work, we discover that attention scores of non-critical blocks exhibit
distributional stability, allowing their contributions to be accurately and efficiently
recovered rather than dropped. Motivated by this insight, we propose PISA, a
training-free Piecewise Sparse Attention that covers the full attention span via
lightweight compensation for skipped non-critical blocks. Unlike the conventional
“keep-or-drop” paradigm that directly drops non-critical blocks, PISA establishes
the “exact-or-approximate” paradigm: it preserves exact computation for critical
blocks while approximating the remainder through block-wise Taylor expansion
around block centroids. This design allows PISA to serve as a faithful proxy to full
attention, achieving 1.91× and 2.57× speedups on Wan2.1 and Hunyuan-Video,
and 1.2–1.6× acceleration on FLUX without compromising quality.

Introduction
Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) [1] have demonstrated impressive performance and scalability in
high-fidelity image and video generation across diverse visual tasks [2–10]. However, as demand for
higher resolutions and longer video durations grows, the sequence length of input tokens increases
dramatically. Consequently, the quadratic complexity of self-attention [11] becomes a significant
bottleneck to efficient inference in large-scale DiTs.
To address the computational bottleneck, especially in high-resolution image and video generation,
recent research has leveraged the inherent sparsity in DiTs to enable sparse attention. Early works [12,
13] capitalized on the spatiotemporal redundancy of video diffusion transformers to introduce static,
training-free sparse attention patterns. To improve adaptability, other methods [14–17] propose
computing sparse patterns dynamically at runtime. Moving beyond training-free approaches, recent
works explore trainable sparse attention [18, 19], adopt linear attention for efficient generation [20, 4,
21], or combine sparse and linear attention [22, 23].
However, existing methods still face inherent limitations: (1) Hard truncation: Sparse attention
directly discards key-value pairs, leading to performance drops at high sparsity and inefficiency on
shorter sequences (e.g., 4K tokens). (2) Incompatibility with pre-trained weights: Linear and hybrid
attention fundamentally alter the attention distribution of pre-trained models, precluding the direct
reuse of weights and necessitating expensive retraining. These limitations underscore the need for a
unified mechanism that enhances efficiency without sacrificing quality or requiring retraining.
To this end, we propose PISA, a novel training-free sparse attention method that accelerates DiTs
while maintaining high accuracy through piecewise computation. Unlike standard sparse attention,
which computes only critical blocks and discards the rest, PISA treats attention as a piecewise
process: (1) Exact computation for sparse key-value blocks to preserve critical information; (2)
Approximation for the remaining blocks using block-wise Taylor expansion to cover the massive
amount of non-critical information.
Specifically, we propose a hybrid-order approximation strategy that uses block-wise zero-order
expansion and global first-order approximation to efficiently improve accuracy. This significantly
improves approximation fidelity to full attention with negligible computational overhead over standard
sparse attention, as illustrated in Figure 1(d). These dual computational pathways are fused into a
single online softmax process, achieving a state-of-the-art trade-off between efficiency and accuracy.
Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of PISA. It accelerates Wan2.1-14B [2] and
Hunyuan-Video [3] by 1.91× and 2.57×, respectively, while preserving state-of-the-art quality. Even
for image generation tasks with lower inherent sparsity, PISA outperforms existing methods in both
efficiency and quality.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
1. We propose a novel training-free piecewise sparse attention that enables full attention span while
substantially reducing computational cost. Through a unified exact-or-approximate computation, it
resolves the critical dilemma between accuracy and efficiency.
2. We develop a hybrid-order approximation scheme that boosts accuracy at negligible cost. We also
derive a covariance-aware routing strategy from error analysis to reduce approximation divergence.
3. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art quality and efficiency across
diverse tasks, setting a new sparse attention paradigm for efficient visual generation.

Related Work
Block Sparse Attention.
To address the quadratic complexity of standard attention [24], sparse
attention [14, 18, 13, 17, 19, 25, 26] limits computation to a subset of critical key-value blocks
via static priors or dynamic routing. However, current methods simply discard the unselected
blocks, which inevitably exacerbates output error and degrades performance at a high sparsity
ratio. In contrast, PISA guarantees a strictly lower error bound than standard sparse attention by
approximating the unselected blocks instead of dropping them, enabling superior performance.

Bidirectional Linear Attention.
Linear attention [27, 28] achieves linear complexity by replacing
the exponential kernel with feature mappings. In the vision domain, existing bidirectional methods
primarily focus on modifying these feature mappings [21, 29, 30], yet their core formulation remains
consistent with the canonical framework. Notably, Taylor-based approaches [31] typically expand
around zero for the entire sequence. Although kernel tricks [32] can yield high-order approximations,
they incur severe dimension explosion and fail to preserve the pre-trained attention distribution.
Consequently, these methods require computationally expensive retraining.
Native Hybrid Attention.
Recognizing the limitations of pure sparse or linear approaches, recent
works have explored hybrid architectures. Methods like SLA [22] and NHA [33] attempt to combine
sparse (or sliding window) attention with linear attention. However, SLA directly sums the outputs of
different branches, which disrupts the intrinsic normalization of attention weights, while NHA adds
linear-RNN memory slots; both require training to adapt pre-trained models. In contrast, PISA applies
block-wise expansion to both the normalization numerator and denominator. By natively mixing
exact and approximate terms under a unified softmax framework instead of simply adding outputs,
our method preserves the intrinsic distribution and enables superior training-free performance.

Key Insights.
To identify a superior alternative to the “keep-or-drop” strategy, we analyzed the
statistical properties of pre-trained models, yielding two key insights:
(1) Pre-softmax scores of uncritical blocks exhibit a symmetric distribution centered around zero

(2) Normalization Consistency. Existing hybrid methods rely on an additive strategy (Osparse +
Olinear) that violates the intrinsic weighted sum rule. To ensure training-free compatibility, the
approximation must be integrated internally into the softmax numerator and denominator.

3.4
Hardware-aware Kernel Implementation
We design a fused kernel which efficiently interleaves exact computation with approximate scanning
in online softmax. Concretely, the kernel proceeds in four phases:
• Prepare Phase: block centroids, block-wise value sums and the global statistic ¯
H are pre-computed
in a single pass, and the critical blocks Si of each query block are selected via Eq. (7).
• Phase 1: the selected blocks j ∈Si are loaded into SRAM for exact sparse attention, updating the
running maximum and normalizer of the online softmax.
• Phase 2: aggregated block vectors are scanned densely in groups with coalesced memory access,
while a dynamic mask excludes selected blocks, enabling high throughput for the tail mass.
• Phase 3: the global first-order term is injected once per query block before the final normalization,
recovering first-order information at negligible cost.
Phases 1–3 form a single fused kernel; the pipeline is illustrated in Figure 3 and Algorithm 1.

Experiments
4.1
Experimental Setup
Models.
We evaluate PISA on both video and image generation tasks. We employ Wan-2.1 [2] and
Hunyuan-Video to produce videos at 480p and 720p resolutions. For image generation, we utilize SD
3.5 [35] and FLUX.1-dev [5] to generate images at 1024×1024 (default) and 2048×2048 resolutions.
Benchmarks.
We employ VBench [36] to evaluate both video quality and consistency. For image
generation, we utilize FID [37] alongside a suite of human preference metrics, including ImageReward
(IR) [38], HPSv2 [39], and MPS [40]. Furthermore, we compute SSIM, PSNR and LPIPS [41] to
quantify the similarity between PISA and full attention. Regarding efficiency, we note that theoretical
FLOPs often fail to reflect real-world speeds. Therefore, to ensure a fair comparison of efficiency, we
focus exclusively on end-to-end latency and the speedup ratio relative to full attention.
Implementation Details.
We define sparsity as the proportion of blocks computed using approxima-
tion, analogous to the ratio of skipped blocks in standard sparse attention. Our kernel implementation
uses a block size of 64×64. Following prior works [19, 17], we adopt a warmup strategy where early
layers and inference steps retain dense. Detailed configurations are provided in the Appendix E.

4.3
Visual Generation Evaluation
Video Generation.
We evaluate our PISA against recent sparse attention works (e.g.,
SpargeAttn [14], SVG [13] and SVG2 [17]) on the Wan2.1 [2] and Hunyuan-Video [3]. As demon-
strated in Table 1, our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on the VBench benchmark,
significantly outperforming existing approaches while delivering substantial speedup advantages. Re-
markably, it even surpasses full attention on certain generation quality metrics. In contrast, although
SVG2 achieves high similarity scores, it suffers from severe degradation in overall video quality and
consistency, exhibiting noticeable blurring and flickering between frames. Similarly, SpargeAttn
struggles with the loss of fine-grained details, whereas PISA exhibits visual quality consistent with
full attention, as shown in Figure 6.
Image Generation.
We compare our method against SpargeAttn [14] and MInference [44] (bidi-
rectional version) on Stable Diffusion 3.5 (SD3.5) [35] and FLUX.1-dev [5], with results shown
in Table 2 (2048×2048 results are in Table 7). At similar sparsity levels, our method significantly
outperforms other methods in FID and other human preference benchmarks, while achieving greater
speedups. In terms of similarity, our method consistently surpasses other methods across different
models, demonstrating that our piecewise attention mechanism preserves critical structural and
semantic information. Visual results are presented in Figure 1 and Appendix G.

4.4
Ablation Study
Approximation Errors.
We examine the normalized L1 error ratio of attention on Wan2.1-1.3B.
As shown in Figure 7, the zeroth-order variant of PISA consistently achieves lower errors than
standard sparse attention, while the hybrid-order variant yields further improvements. This validates
the numerical precision of our method, confirming its ability to minimize output error.
Effect of Approximation Strategy.
Beyond output error of attention, we evaluate generation
similarity against the exact block-wise first-order variant (Eq. (3)). As shown in Table 3, our hybrid
approximation significantly improves similarity over the zeroth-order method and approaches the
exact baseline with superior efficiency, demonstrating an optimal fidelity-efficiency trade-off.
Qualitative Comparison.
We visualized the approximation effects on FLUX.1 in Figure 8. While
the 0th-order expansion maintains semantics and structural integrity, it struggles with local details. In
contrast, the hybrid strategy significantly recovers these fine-grained details. This provides intuitive
visual evidence that hybrid strategy effectively preserves human-perceptible details.
5
Conclusion
In this work, we propose a unified “exact-or-approximate” framework that goes beyond the standard
“keep-or-drop” sparse attention paradigm and achieves a better efficiency-accuracy trade-off for visual
generation. Our current scope focuses on multi-step diffusion models with dense warmup. Future
work will explore the co-design of PISA with few-step diffusion [45–48], advanced sampling [49, 50],
and feature caching [51, 52], as well as its application to large language models (LLMs).
