Abstract
We propose loom attention, which allows a Trans-
former to dynamically rewire its circuits at every
token by interweaving a head’s vertical attention
over the outputs of earlier heads (to obtain its
query, key and value) with its horizontal atten-
tion over tokens. We instantiate vertical attention
efficiently with normalized and gated linear at-
tention over a per-token matrix memory, which
stores the compressed head outputs and serves
as a second residual stream. This yields Loom-
Former, a dual-stream architecture that splits con-
trol and content between the vector and matrix
streams. Across model sizes and training token
budgets in language modeling and downstream
tasks, LoomFormer matches Transformers trained
with over 3× compute and recent residual-stream
baselines (HC/mHC, MUDDFormer, AttnRes)
trained with ~1.7–2.8×. For example, trained
on the same 300B tokens with only 2% extra
FLOPs, LoomPythia-2.8B achieves ~1.6× the av-
erage downstream gain of mHC-Pythia-2.8B over
Pythia-2.8B, and matches (0-shot) or surpasses
(5-shot) Pythia-12B.
1. Introduction
Transformers solve tasks by composing attention heads
across layers into circuits (Elhage et al., 2020; Olsson et al.,
2022; Wang et al., 2023; Merullo et al., 2024): an earlier
head writes a feature into a subspace of the residual stream,
and a later head reads from that subspace into its query, key
or value. Induction heads, for example, read the output of
a previous-token head through their keys (K-composition)
(Olsson et al., 2022).
The composition between two heads, however, is determined
by the product of their output and input projections, a virtual
weight that does not change after training (Elhage et al.,
2020). Attention makes the horizontal flow of information
across tokens input-dependent: a head chooses which tokens
to attend to. In contrast, the vertical flow across layers

A model that needs different circuits for different inputs
therefore keeps all of them wired at once, lets them compete
for the residual stream, and can gate them only indirectly,
e.g., by sending a head’s attention to a sink token (Xiao et al.,
2024b). We ask whether the wiring itself can be chosen per
token, i.e., whether circuits can be made rewirable.
Recent work has enriched the vertical flow. Dense connec-
tions let a layer aggregate the outputs of all earlier layers
(Pagliardini et al., 2024); MUDDFormer (Xiao et al., 2025)
and Attention Residuals (AttnRes) (Kimi Team, 2026a)
make this aggregation input-dependent, turning it into a
depth-wise (i.e., vertical) attention (separate for the query,

key, value and residual inputs in MUDDFormer); and Hyper-
Connections (HC) (Zhu et al., 2025) and its manifold-
constrained variant mHC (Xie et al., 2025) maintain several
residual streams with learnable or dynamic mixing. In all of
them, however, the vertical channel meets horizontal atten-
tion only through the hidden state of a layer, in which the
outputs of all heads have already been summed and mixed
together: a head can neither single out a particular earlier
head nor be singled out by a later one. The vertical and
horizontal flows run side by side, never woven together.
To make circuits rewirable, we propose loom attention, in
which a head chooses which earlier heads to read from
just as it chooses which tokens to attend to. By attending
vertically over the outputs of these heads at the same token,
the head obtains its query, key and value to perform the
standard horizontal attention over tokens, and its output
is in turn attended to vertically by later heads. Vertical
and horizontal attention are thus interwoven, each feeding
the other at every head, like the warp and weft of a loom
(Figure 1).
Naive vertical attention over the outputs of all earlier heads
is, however, expensive. We therefore instantiate it with nor-
malized and gated linear attention over a per-token matrix
memory, which stores the compressed head outputs and
serves as a second residual stream. We call the resulting
dual-stream architecture LoomFormer, after a drawloom—a
loom in which a separate stored pattern selects which warp
threads to lift at each pick, rather than repeating one fixed
pattern. 1 Here the ordinary vector stream plays the pattern,
carrying control—which memory slots each head writes
and reads (weaving a different circuit per token) as well
as which tokens to attend to—while the matrix stream car-
ries the content moved across tokens. The two streams are
coupled through attention outputs and FFN writes.
Extensive experiments on language modeling and down-
stream tasks across model sizes and training token budgets
show that LoomFormer adds only 2.0–3.7% FLOPs while
matching Transformers trained with over 3× compute and
recent residual-stream baselines (HC/mHC, MUDDFormer,
AttnRes) trained with ~1.7–2.8×. For example, trained on
the same 300B tokens, LoomPythia-2.8B achieves ~1.6× the
average downstream gain of mHC-Pythia-2.8B over Pythia-
2.8B, and matches (0-shot) or surpasses (5-shot) Pythia-
12B (Figure 2). We also analyze the trained 2.8B models
to understand how and why LoomFormer works—e.g., by
rewiring head outputs to inputs per token, it keeps over three
times as many deep-layer heads off attention sinks as Pythia
and mHC.

Gating a writer or a reader acts on its whole row or column
of C, so a wiring pattern is realized only if it is such a
rectangle, as in the first column of Figure 3; other patterns
fail in one of two ways. With dense virtual weights, where
heads share subspaces, every pair can be connected, but a
non-rectangular pattern such as {W1 →R1, W3 →R3}
(last column) also connects W1 →R3 and W3 →R1: the
two circuits interfere. With sparse virtual weights, where
groups of heads use separate subspaces, circuits in different
groups do not interfere, but pairs across groups such as
W3 →R2 (second column) are unreachable.
The price of rewiring by node gating.
Can a node-gated
architecture overcome this by spending more heads? Sup-
pose the role of each of r ≥2 readers may be played by
several heads, gated on and off per token, and at every token
each reader must receive exactly one of w writers, chosen in-
dependently of the other readers; the last pattern in Figure 3
is the smallest instance. Let N(w, t) denote the minimum
size of a ground set carrying a t-cover-free family of w sets,
i.e., w sets none of which is covered by the union of t others
(Kautz & Singleton, 1964).
Theorem 2.4. To realize all such wiring patterns, a node-
gated architecture needs at least N(w, r −1) heads per
reader, and this number suffices, where N(w, 1) ≈log2 w,
N(w, t) = Ω(t2 log w/ log t) for 2 ≤t ≤√w and
N(w, t) = Θ(w) for t > √w.
Intuitively, a head receives every writer that is on and has a
nonzero virtual weight to it, so a reader must hold in reserve
a head that sees its chosen writer and none of the up to
r −1 writers chosen by the other readers (Theorem A.2
in Section A.2; bounds in Section A.3). With the r = 3n
readers of a layer choosing among its w = nl earlier writers,
r −1 > √w whenever l ≤9n −6, so at practical sizes
node gating needs Θ(w) heads per reader.

Proposition 3.1. (i) With ports fixed to (K, Q), Loom-
Former is a standard Transformer TfmK,Q with residual
stream (x, vec(M)) ∈RD+dkd, whose virtual weights ab-
sorb KQ⊤, hence node-gated. (ii) With ports generated per
token, LoomFormer is rewirable with one head per reader
w.r.t. the wiring patterns with at most dk writers, whatever
the total number of writers w.
In (ii), only the writers in the pattern are on, each taking a
slot through a one-hot key while each reader’s query selects
the slots of its writers (Section A.6). The N(w, r −1) heads
per reader that node gating needs (Theorem 2.4) are thus
replaced by a per-token assignment of dk slots; when more
than dk writers are on, slots are shared and writers interfere,
as in linear attention. By (i), this rewiring capacity comes
from generating ports per token, not from the matrix mem-
ory itself. Together, (i) and (ii) give a model-level view of
LoomFormer’s expressiveness: within each token, Loom-
Former wires heads as TfmK(x),Q(x), selecting per token
one Transformer from the continuum {TfmK,Q} much as
a mixture of experts selects experts (Fedus et al., 2022),
except that the experts are wirings, share all other weights,
and cost only dk numbers per port to select.

5. Experiments
We train all models on the Pile dataset (Gao et al., 2020)
in JAX, initializing dynamic port gates near zero so train-
ing starts smoothly from static ports (Section D.1). We
evaluate scaling laws (Section 5.1), large-scale pretraining

6. Conclusion
Viewing Transformer circuits through their dynamic wiring
matrix shows that standard Transformers and layer-wise
residual-stream mechanisms are restricted to low-rank gat-
ing, requiring many heads per reader to avoid circuit inter-
ference. Loom attention lifts this bottleneck by interweav-
ing vertical linear attention over a per-token matrix mem-
ory with horizontal attention over tokens, making circuits
rewirable per token at one head per reader and 2.0%–3.7%
extra forward FLOPs. Across model scales and 300B-token
pretraining, LoomFormer matches Transformers trained
with over 3× compute and recent residual-stream baselines
with ~1.7–2.8×, while keeping over three quarters of its
attention heads on and rewiring its ports from token to to-
ken. Because the matrix memory operates along depth at
each token, combining loom attention with linear or sparse
attention along the sequence is a natural direction for future
foundation models.

common min(dk, 3n)-dimensional subspace.

The same form describes linear attention along the sequence instead of depth: the writers and readers are
one head at different tokens, M is the head’s private state, the ports are its keys and queries, and the update Φ distinguishes
the variants—I for linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020), a scalar decay in Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024), a gated
diagonal in gated linear attention (Yang et al., 2024a), I −βk⊤k in DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024b), and γ(I −βk⊤k) in
Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2025). It differs from LoomFormer in the axis and the sharing of the state (Section 4).

B. Extended Related Work
Cross-layer connections and residual-stream architectures.
Beyond standard residual connections (He et al., 2016),
early work strengthened depth-wise signal propagation through input-dependent gating in Highway Networks (Srivastava
et al., 2015) and all-to-all layer aggregation with static or learnable weights in DenseNet (Huang et al., 2017), DLCL (Wang
et al., 2019) and DenseFormer (Pagliardini et al., 2024). Subsequent architectures introduced cross-layer attention over
earlier layer representations (ElNokrashy et al., 2022; Fang et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Kimi Team, 2026a), multi-stream
residual states with dynamic or manifold-constrained mixing in HC and mHC (Zhu et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2025), and
decoupled Q/K/V/residual layer aggregation in MUDDFormer (Xiao et al., 2025). As analyzed in Section 4 and Section A.7,
all of these mechanisms operate at the granularity of whole layers (or layer × Q/K/V), summing the outputs of all heads
within a layer before routing and restricting the within-token wiring rank to 1 (or 3).
The Residual Matrix Transformer (RMT) (Mak & Flanigan, 2025) replaces the vector residual stream entirely with a pure
matrix residual stream, which heads and FFNs read and write through static key vectors so the stream can be widened
cheaply. LoomFormer instead keeps a dual-stream architecture that separates control on the vector stream from content
on the matrix stream: the compact vector stream generates per-token ports cheaply, whereas RMT’s static ports fall under
Theorem 3.1(i) and remain node-gated however wide the matrix is made (4D in RMT’s main configuration vs. 2D + D in
LoomFormer).
Cross-layer token attention and KV sharing.
OmniNet (Tay et al., 2021) lets every token attend directly to all tokens
across all layers, incurring attention cost quadratic in LT; loom attention instead factors cross-layer routing within each
token through a fixed-size matrix memory M in O(LTDdk) time and composes it with standard within-layer horizontal
attention. Cross-Layer Attention (CLA) (Brandon et al., 2024) shares key-value caches across adjacent layers to reduce
inference memory at a modest quality trade-off, whereas loom attention dynamically composes earlier head outputs into
distinct per-head queries, keys and values at every layer (Section 5.3).
Mechanistic interpretability and intra-layer advances.
Circuit-level interpretability studies (Elhage et al., 2020; Olsson
et al., 2022; Conmy et al., 2023; Hanna et al., 2024; Merullo et al., 2024; Ni et al., 2025) show that Transformers implement
tasks by composing specific upstream and downstream heads across layers; loom attention turns this implicit, static
composition into an explicit, per-token operation over M. Orthogonal intra-layer advances—including improved horizontal
attention (Liu et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024a; Ye et al., 2024; Leviathan et al., 2024), sequence-axis linear attention
and state-space models (Gu & Dao, 2024; Dao & Gu, 2024; Peng et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024b; 2025), and sparse
Mixture-of-Experts FFNs (Fedus et al., 2022; Dai et al., 2024)—modify horizontal mixing or FFN capacity within a layer
and can be combined directly with loom attention along depth.

Parameter budget and tokenwise projections.
The reference Transformer has 4D2 attention and 8D2 FFN weights per
layer, for a total of P0 = 12LD2 (applying to both a GELU FFN of hidden width 4D and a SwiGLU FFN of hidden width
8D/3). LoomFormer replaces the full Q/K/V projections with smaller RoPE projections and matrix reads, redistributing
the saved parameter budget among the port generators, FFNx and FFNM, as well as the initial value projection W E and
embedding MWrite (Eq. 10), so that PLoom = P0.
Every tokenwise dense matrix W costs 2T|W| FLOPs, where |W| is its number of entries. Consequently, to leading order,
the combined projection and FFN cost is 2TP0 in both models. This includes both stages of the low-rank port generators,
all projected gates (including W S

port-generation or FFNM FLOPs without subtracting the FFNx FLOPs that paid for their weights would double-count them.
Static ports, learned normalization scales and biases give only lower-order corrections to this dense-weight relation, and for
the same reason the bottleneck width Dr affects parameter allocation without entering the leading net FLOP overhead.
