ABSTRACT
Unified multimodal models aim to support visual understanding and generation
within a single framework. However, prevailing approaches often separate their
visual representations or backbone computation to accommodate the different re-
quirements of these two tasks, which limits the degree of unification. In this pa-
per, we present HIUNI, which revisits this design choice and explores whether a
pretrained vision language model can be directly extended into a unified model
with extensive parameter sharing. Specifically, HIUNI reuses the same language-
aligned ViT and dense Transformer backbone for both capabilities, while confin-
ing task-specific computation to the output side. Yet such a design introduces two
key challenges. Generation in pixel space is difficult to optimize, and semantic
abstraction in pretrained ViT discards fine spatial details required for synthesis.
To address these challenges, HIUNI introduces a Pixel Diffusion Head tailored to
direct pixel-space optimization and a lightweight HiGate pathway that provides
fine-grained spatial details near the output stage. Extensive experiments show
that HIUNI achieves a similar average understanding score to the pretrained VLM
while attaining competitive performance on image generation and editing. Con-
trolled comparisons and analyses further demonstrate that a single dense backbone
can effectively support both capabilities without task-specific backbone computa-
tion, highlighting a simpler path toward unified multimodal modeling.

INTRODUCTION
Image understanding translates visual content into language, whereas visual generation synthesizes
images from textual descriptions. An ideal unified multimodal model (UMM) is expected to support
both capabilities within a single framework and facilitate cross-task interaction between them (Kang
et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026). However, most existing UMMs still maintain two separate visual rep-
resentation spaces with dedicated encoders. The understanding branch extracts high-level semantics
via a language-aligned Vision Transformer (ViT), while the generation branch learns fine-grained
spatial details with a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). As highlighted by Janus (Wu et al., 2025b),
forcing two divergent encoding objectives into one representation triggers severe task interference.
Bagel (Deng et al., 2025) further introduces a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that sep-
arates task-specific computation within the backbone, establishing a modular paradigm adopted by
recent studies (Agarwal et al., 2026; Diao et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2026).
Although this separation preserves individual performance, it limits direct interaction through shared
visual representations. Recent studies further suggest that closer sharing of visual representations
can promote stronger interaction between the two capabilities with an appropriate backbone de-
sign (Zheng et al., 2025; Su et al., 2026). These observations motivate us to revisit whether such
architectural separation is indeed necessary: Can a shared visual space internally harmonize under-
standing and generation without relying on task-specific backbone computation?
Recent advances offer promising opportunities to address this question using a single visual encoder
for both understanding and generation (Liu et al., 2026b; Zhang et al., 2026a). Conventional genera-
tion models heavily rely on VAE latents, yet these features are optimized primarily for reconstruction
and are less aligned with the high-level semantics required for understanding (Wu et al., 2025e; Ma
et al., 2026a). Conversely, modern pixel-space diffusion models demonstrate that high-quality gen-
eration can also be achieved without VAE latents (Li & He, 2026; Yu et al., 2026), opening a new

path towards shared visual representations. Going one step further, some encoder-free architectures
process raw pixels for both understanding and generation (Liu et al., 2026a; Diao et al., 2026), but
the absence of pretrained visual semantic priors makes optimization substantially less stable and less
data efficient. An alternative choice is to reuse language-aligned ViT as the shared visual encoder,
given its strong semantic representations and direct compatibility with standard Vision Language
Model (VLM) infrastructures. Nevertheless, adapting such an architecture to image generation in-
troduces a spatial bottleneck. VLMs typically employ a patch merger after ViT to compress visual
tokens, doubling effective patch size from 14/16 to 28/32 (Bai et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). Such
aggressive spatial pooling, together with the semantic abstraction of deep ViT layers, improves un-
derstanding efficiency, but weakens fine-grained information needed for high-fidelity synthesis.
Following this direction, we introduce HIUNI, a UMM initialized from a pretrained VLM that ex-
tends image understanding to generation through a shared semantic visual pathway. Understanding
and generation use the same visual encoder, token representation, and dense backbone. Task-specific
computation is confined to the output modules. For image generation, we observe optimization insta-
bility when applying a standard DiT to pixel-space diffusion in our early experiments. We therefore
design a dedicated Pixel Diffusion Head with a simplified modulation for pixel-space generation. To
compensate for spatial information weakened by patch merging and deep semantic abstraction, we
incorporate a lightweight gating module (HiGate), inspired by Cheers (Zhang et al., 2026b), which
directly routes uncompressed pixel-level features to the pixel diffusion head. This residual pathway
supplies local spatial cues for image generation while leaving semantic modeling in the shared visual
encoder and backbone.
We comprehensively validate HIUNI on image understanding, image generation, and image editing.
HIUNI retains strong visual understanding capabilities inherited from the pretrained VLM while in-
corporating image generation into the shared backbone. It also achieves the highest overall Chinese
score on Qwen-Image-Bench among the compared UMMs, together with competitive image edit-
ing performance. More importantly, further architectural comparison reveals the trade-offs between
shared and task-specific backbone computation. While task-routed MoT improves generation over a
same-scale dense backbone under matched training settings, a larger shared dense backbone with a
comparable total parameter count achieves substantially stronger understanding while maintaining
competitive generation. These results suggest that scaling a shared dense backbone within a shared
visual space offers a simple and promising path toward unified multimodal modeling.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose HIUNI, which provides a new perspective on building UMMs from pretrained
VLMs. Understanding, generation, and editing share the same language-aligned encoder
and dense backbone, while task-specific computation is limited to the output module.
• We develop a Pixel Diffusion Head tailored to direct pixel-space generation, together with
a lightweight HiGate pathway that complements the shared semantic representation with
fine-grained spatial details.
• Extensive experiments show that HIUNI maintains strong understanding while achieving
competitive generation and editing performance. Controlled comparisons further highlight
that a shared dense backbone has the potential to effectively support both understanding
and generation without task-specific backbone computation.

RELATED WORK
Unified Multimodal Models. UMMs are intended to support visual understanding and generation
within a single model. Existing UMMs mainly follow two paradigms according to how images
are represented and generated. Autoregressive models tokenize images into discrete visual tokens
and model text and vision under a unified autoregressive objective (Team, 2024; Cui et al., 2025; Wu
et al., 2025e; Team et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026b). This formulation provides a simple and scalable
interface across modalities, but discrete tokenization may lose subtle visual details required for high
fidelity image synthesis. Another line of work follows the Transfusion paradigm (Zhou et al., 2025),
which retains the autoregressive prediction of language while applying diffusion or flow matching
to continuous visual features for image generation (Deng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026b; Yan et al.,
2026). More recently, pixel-space diffusion further demonstrates that strong image synthesis can be
achieved without VAE latents (Li & He, 2026; Yu et al., 2026), and local details can be recovered
from noisy pixels to complement global semantic modeling (Chen et al., 2026b; Ma et al., 2026b),
opening a more direct path toward shared visual representations. Together, these advances shift
an important architectural question toward how visual representations and backbone computation
should be shared between understanding and generation.
Visual Unification. The architectural design of UMMs depends on how visual representations and
backbone computation are organized. Many existing models maintain separate visual spaces due to
the different requirements of understanding and generation, where a language-aligned ViT provides
semantic features for understanding, and a reconstruction-oriented VAE preserves fine-grained de-
tails for generation (Wu et al., 2025b; Deng et al., 2025). To reduce this separation, recent studies
explore more unified visual pathways. TUNA cascades a VAE encoder with a representation encoder
to construct a shared visual representation, while TUNA-2 further removes both encoders for pixel
space modeling (Liu et al., 2026b;a). Cheers decouples semantic representations and local details
through a unified visual tokenizer and gated detail residuals in generation head (Zhang et al., 2026b).
HYDRA adopts a progressive ViT, and UniSpace introduces reconstruction aware patch represen-
tations into a pretrained ViT (Zhang et al., 2026a; Yan et al., 2026). These designs reduce visual
separation, but differ in their use of pretrained visual encoders and task-specific backbone computa-
tion. For example, UniSpace still adopts task-specific Transformer experts, while encoder-free mod-
els such as SenseNova remove both ViT and VAE but retain an MoT backbone (Yan et al., 2026;
Diao et al., 2026). In another related direction, RepFusion processes noisy visual representations
through a pretrained VLM, but relies on a separate diffusion Transformer for representation-space
generation (Pan et al., 2026). HIUNI reuses the ViT and patch merger of a pretrained VLM and
shares their output representation and a single dense backbone across understanding and generation,
without separate generative visual encoders or task-specific backbone computation.
3
METHOD
Image understanding and generation differ substantially in their input and output forms, but both
rely on semantic alignment between visual content and language. HIUNI therefore separates shared
semantic modeling from task-specific prediction. Specifically, we seek to let the visual encoder and
multimodal backbone provide a common semantic pathway for both capabilities, while introducing
generation-specific processing only when detailed visual signals need to be reconstructed. This
design allows understanding and generation to share the visual and backbone computation without
requiring separate semantic representations.
Following this principle, HIUNI is built upon a pretrained VLM and extends it with image generation
capability while reusing its pretrained visual encoder and backbone architecture. Both understanding
and generation use the same language-aligned ViT, the same visual tokens after patch merging, and
the same dense backbone. Textual outputs are predicted by the native language modeling head, while
image synthesis is handled by a new Pixel Diffusion Head. No separate semantic visual encoder or
task-specific backbone is involved. To compensate for the spatial information weakened by patch
merging and deep semantic abstraction, HIUNI further adopts a lightweight gating module, termed
HiGate, which routes raw features from the noisy image to the diffusion head. The residual branch
supplies local spatial cues to complement the shared semantic representation without modifying the
main semantic computation. Figure 2 illustrates the overall architecture.

3.1
UNIFIED MULTIMODAL ARCHITECTURE
Shared Visual Encoding.
HIUNI adopts a language aligned ViT from the pretrained VLM as the
only visual encoder for both understanding and generation. Let E denote the ViT and M denote the
patch merger, which compresses the ViT outputs into visual tokens. For an input image x, the visual
tokens are obtained as Z = M(E(x)). The same pathway is used for clean images in understanding
and visual conditioning, as well as noisy target images in generation. Consequently, both tasks are
grounded in the same visual token space without requiring separate or task-specific encoders.
Shared Dense Backbone.
The shared visual tokens are further processed by the same dense back-
bone for both understanding and generation. Text embeddings and visual tokens are organized into
a multimodal sequence S and processed by a single dense Transformer B, yielding H = B(S).
Attention mask allows bidirectional interaction within each image, while text tokens retain causal
attention for autoregressive prediction. After the shared backbone, task-specific prediction is han-
dled on the output side. For image understanding, the language modeling head projects hidden states
corresponding to textual outputs onto the vocabulary space. For image generation, hidden states at
the target visual positions are passed to the pixel diffusion head for image prediction.
3.2
PIXEL-SPACE IMAGE GENERATION
Pixel Diffusion Head.
Diffusion directly in pixel space poses additional optimization challenges
compared with conventional latent diffusion, as the model must predict high dimensional pixel sig-
nals while maintaining semantic consistency and fine spatial structure (Jiang et al., 2026; Li & He,
2026). Consistent with these observations, we observe frequent loss spikes when using a standard
DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) with repeated AdaLN modulation in our early experiments. In subse-
quent development runs, a simplified design without block-wise AdaLN exhibits smoother training
trajectories. Motivated by this observation, we remove AdaLN from the DiT blocks, reducing the
diffusion head to a standard Transformer architecture.
Timestep conditioning enters through an explicit time token and a final modulation layer. Inspired
by recent works (Ma et al., 2025; Team, 2025), for timestep t, a sinusoidal encoding ϕ(t) is mapped
by a two layer MLP to a time embedding et ∈RDb, where Db denotes the hidden dimension of
the backbone. The resulting vector replaces the embedding of the <time> token and is processed
together with the multimodal sequence by the shared backbone. The backbone hidden states for the
target visual positions, together with the time token representation, are projected to the diffusion
hidden dimension D and processed by N Transformer blocks.

At the output of the diffusion head, we retain a single timestep modulation before pixel prediction. A
separate two layer MLP maps the same sinusoidal encoding ϕ(t) to a diffusion time embedding ct ∈
RD. This embedding is further projected to channel wise scale and shift parameters as [γt, βt] =
Wmod SiLU(ct) + bmod. Let Fout ∈RNg×D denote the target visual features at the output of the
diffusion head. The final modulation is given by ¯Fout = (1+γt)⊙LN(Fout)+βt. After modulation,
a linear projection predicts the corresponding pixel patches. With a ViT patch size of 16 and a spatial
merge factor of 2, each generated token predicts a 32 × 32 RGB region, corresponding to a flattened
patch dimension of Np = 3×32×32. Finally, the predicted patches are rearranged into image space
to obtain ˆx0. In this way, the head removes repeated block-wise AdaLN while preserving timestep
conditioning through the shared backbone and final modulation layer.
HiGate.
Although the unified visual tokens carry strong semantics, the patch merger reduces their
spatial resolution, while deeper ViT layers increasingly favor abstraction over local texture. Such
compression is well suited to understanding but may discard cues important for pixel-level recon-
struction. To complement the semantic pathway, HIUNI introduces a lightweight HiGate module,
inspired by Cheers (Zhang et al., 2026b), which directly injects low-level pixel information into the
diffusion head without introducing an additional visual encoder.
Specifically, the noisy image xt ∈R3×H×W branches into two pathways before ViT patch em-
bedding. The main pathway encodes xt through the ViT, patch merger, and the shared backbone
to obtain semantic features, while a lightweight residual branch directly preserves its local pixel
content. In the residual branch, each 2 × 2 group of 16 × 16 RGB patch is concatenated into
Rt ∈RNg×Np, following the same spatial layout as the target visual tokens. A linear projection
maps these raw pixel features to the diffusion hidden dimension D, producing Pt ∈RNg×D. The
aligned pixel features Pt are then fused with the target features Fg ∈RNg×D through HiGate. A
lightweight MLP predicts a token wise gate gt ∈RNg×1, and the residual fusion is formulated as
eFg = LN(Fg+gt⊙LN(Pt)). Only the target visual positions are updated by this residual injection,
and the resulting features continue through the Pixel Diffusion Head. In this manner, the residual
pathway supplies fine-grained spatial cues for image synthesis, while high-level semantic modeling
remains in the unified ViT and shared backbone.
3.3
TASK FORMULATION AND JOINT TRAINING
Unified Task Formulation.
HIUNI supports text-to-image generation and image editing through
the same generation pathway, with the two tasks differing only in their conditioning context. For
image generation, the condition is a textual prompt p. For image editing, the condition consists of
an editing instruction p and a reference image xr. The reference image follows the same visual
encoding pathway as the target image and is processed together with the noisy target image by the
shared backbone. Formally, we denote the conditioning context by Cτ, where τ ∈{t2i, edit},
Ct2i = p, and Cedit = (p, xr). Given Cτ and the noisy target image xt, both tasks follow the same
generation pathway without introducing task specific branches.
Joint Training Objectives.
HIUNI jointly optimizes language modeling, image understanding
and image generation within the same backbone. Textual outputs for language modeling and im-
age understanding are trained with the standard next token prediction objective. Let C denote the
input context, the language modeling objective is Ltxt = −1

EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate HIUNI on image understanding, image generation, and image editing, and further exam-
ine architectural design through controlled comparisons. Additional training and evaluation details,
analyses of pixel diffusion optimization stability and cross task interaction, an extension to chain of
thought image generation, and qualitative results are provided in Appendices A-F.
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Implementation Details.
We initialize HIUNI from Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (Bai et al., 2025),
reusing its language model backbone as the shared dense backbone and its language aligned ViT
as the shared visual encoder. The multimodal training corpus covers image understanding, image
generation, and image editing, with their category distributions summarized in Figure 3. Following
the three-stage procedure in Section 3.3, we first warm up the Pixel Diffusion Head and HiGate for
30K steps on image generation data while freezing all pretrained components. We then unfreeze the
full model and perform 310K steps of joint pretraining on pure text modeling, image understanding,
and image generation, with the generation resolution progressively increased from 256 × 256 to
2048×2048. Finally, we conduct 22K steps of supervised fine tuning, where image editing is added
while the existing tasks are retained. The learning rate is 1 × 10−4 during generation warmup and
early joint pretraining, and is reduced to 2 × 10−5 in later pretraining, while supervised fine tuning
uses a peak learning rate of 2 × 10−5. The complete phase-wise recipe, including training steps,
batch sizes, learning rate schedules, image resolutions, loss weights, and task sampling ratios, is
provided in Appendix A and Table 6.
Baselines.
We compare HIUNI with a broad set of UMMs and specialized models. Representative
UMM baselines include Harmon (Wu et al., 2025d), Cheers (Zhang et al., 2026b), InternVL-U (Tian
et al., 2026), Janus-Pro (Chen et al., 2025), Show-o2 (Xie et al., 2026b), TUNA (Liu et al., 2026b),
Bagel (Deng et al., 2025), SenseNova-U1 (Diao et al., 2026), UniSpace (Yan et al., 2026) among oth-
ers. For image understanding, we also report Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (Bai et al., 2025), from which
HIUNI is initialized, as a reference for the image understanding capability of the pretrained VLM.
For image generation, we further compare against strong baselines such as GLM-Image (GLM-
Image Team, 2026), Qwen-Image (Wu et al., 2025a), and HiDream-O1 (Cai et al., 2026). For
image editing, we include recent specialized models Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Qwen Team, 2025),

MAIN RESULTS
Image Understanding.
We evaluate HIUNI on eight representative benchmarks, including Math-
Vista (Lu et al., 2024), MMBench (Liu et al., 2024a), RealWorldQA (AI, 2024), MMStar (Chen
et al., 2024), HallusionBench (Guan et al., 2024), AI2D (Kembhavi et al., 2016), OCRBench (Liu
et al., 2024b), and OmniDocBench (Ouyang et al., 2025). As shown in Table 1, HIUNI achieves
strong performance across the evaluated understanding benchmarks. Across the seven higher-is-
better benchmarks, HIUNI achieves an average score of 75.56, close to the officially reported 75.61
of Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, while maintaining the same OmniDocBench score of 0.24. Compared
with the re-evaluated Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking baseline under the same evaluation settings, HIUNI
achieves better results on four of eight benchmarks. Performance changes are benchmark dependent,
with improvements on benchmarks such as MathVista and HallusionBench and declines on others
such as MMBench and OCRBench. Despite its relatively compact model scale, HIUNI achieves
understanding performance comparable to several substantially larger UMMs across the evaluated
benchmarks. Overall, these results indicate that strong understanding performance can coexist with
image generation in the proposed shared architecture.
Image Generation.
We evaluate HIUNI on Qwen-Image-Bench (Li et al., 2026), OneIG (Chang
et al., 2026), and LongText (Geng et al., 2025). These benchmarks provide complementary evalua-
tions that cover perceptual quality, aesthetics, semantic alignment, realism, creativity, and the ability
to render long text within images. From Table 2, we can observe that HIUNI achieves the highest
Chinese score among UMMs on Qwen-Image-Bench, while remaining competitive on the English
split. For OneIG and LongText, HIUNI also stays close to the strongest unified baselines, indicat-
ing strong performance on both general image generation and long text rendering. Together, these
results show that HIUNI achieves strong generation capability within a UMM architecture.

4.3
TRADE-OFFS BETWEEN SHARED AND TASK-SPECIFIC COMPUTATION
Experimental Configurations.
In this section, we examine whether understanding and generation
can be effectively supported by a shared dense backbone. We compare three configurations, HIUNI-
Dense-2B, HIUNI-MoT-2B, and HIUNI-Dense-4B. HIUNI-Dense-2B and HIUNI-MoT-2B are both
initialized from Qwen3-VL-2B. For HIUNI-MoT-2B, the pretrained backbone is duplicated to ini-
tialize separate understanding and generation branches. These two models follow the same training
recipe and differ only in whether the backbone is shared or task-routed, which both undergo 30K
warmup steps followed by 30K pretraining steps. Moreover, HIUNI-Dense-4B is initialized from
Qwen3-VL-4B and is included as an additional reference for allocating a similar total parameter
budget to a larger shared backbone. We further introduce two single-task baselines, HIUNI-Und-4B
and HIUNI-Gen-4B, which share the same architecture and Qwen3-VL-4B initialization as HIUNI-
Dense-4B, but are trained exclusively on understanding and generation data, respectively, with the
same training steps. For a fair comparison, we adopt the same 0.5B diffusion head across all config-
urations, with its hidden dimension aligned to the 2B backbone.
Results and Analysis.
As shown in Table 4, compared with HIUNI-MoT-2B, HIUNI-Dense-2B
achieves comparable understanding performance (58.55 vs. 59.23) with a fully shared backbone,
while its OneIG score decreases from 0.345 to 0.291. This comparison suggests that task-specific
backbone computation benefits image generation under the same training settings, although a shared
dense backbone can also support both capabilities. Under a similar total parameter budget, HI-
UNI-Dense-4B outperforms HIUNI-MoT-2B on all six understanding benchmarks, while retaining
competitive generation performance. Notably, the shared design allows HIUNI-Dense-4B to inherit
stronger pretrained representations from Qwen3-VL-4B, whereas HIUNI-MoT-2B has to distribute
its capacity across two branches initialized from Qwen3-VL-2B. Within the dense architecture, scal-
ing from 2B to 4B improves the understanding average by 11.1% and the OneIG score by 7.9%,
suggesting that a larger shared backbone can benefit both capabilities.
When comparing with the single-task baselines, HIUNI-Dense-4B achieves comparable understand-
ing performance to HIUNI-Und-4B (65.04 vs. 64.58), suggesting that incorporating image gener-
ation does not substantially compromise understanding. Notably, MMBench remains comparable,
while OCRBench shows only a modest decrease, which suggest that the performance gaps relative
to the pretrained VLM cannot be solely attributed to joint modeling and may also be influenced by
the training data distribution. Meanwhile, HIUNI-Gen-4B achieves a OneIG score of 0.390 demon-
strating the feasibility of extending a pretrained VLM to image generation by reusing its visual

4.4
ANALYSIS OF RESIDUAL VISUAL PATHWAY
Residual Feature Source.
The shared ViT and patch merger provide a common semantic rep-
resentation for understanding and generation, but they also reduce fine-grained spatial information
before the diffusion head. We therefore examine which feature source is more suitable for the resid-
ual pathway used in HIUNI. Specifically, we compare three configurations: directly using pixel-level
features before patch embedding (Pixel Features), injecting features from the ViT (ViT Features),
or even removing the residual pathway (No Gate). All other architectural and training settings are
kept fixed. As shown in Table 5, both residual sources improve image generation over No Gate.
The average OneIG score increases from 0.267 to 0.284 with ViT features and further to 0.314 with
pixel-level features. Compared with ViT features, pixel-level features provide an additional gain
of 0.030, suggesting that features closer to the pixel space better complement the semantic repre-
sentation produced by the shared ViT. Meanwhile, the average understanding score remains almost
unchanged across the three settings, ranging from 65.04 to 65.17. These results support our choice
of directly injecting pixel-level information into the generation pathway of HIUNI.
Residual Injection Strength.
We further analyze how HIUNI uses the pixel-level residual path-
way during image generation. Figure 5 visualizes the learned gate strength over diffusion timesteps
and training checkpoints. For each timestep, we compute the maximum gate magnitude and normal-
ize it by the global maximum over all plotted timesteps and checkpoints. The gate remains weak at
high noise levels and gradually becomes stronger as the input approaches the clean image, suggest-
ing that the residual pathway contributes mainly at later denoising stages where fine-grained spatial
details are more important. The overall gate strength also decreases as pretraining progresses, and
early checkpoints rely more on the residual pathway. Combined with the residual feature source
analysis above, these results suggest that HIUNI can retain semantic modeling in the shared back-
bone while supplying generation-specific spatial details near the output stage.

CROSS-TASK INTERACTION
Beyond architectural sharing, we further examine whether supervision from one visual task affects
the others under the shared backbone. We chose image editing, as it requires both visual under-
standing and generation capabilities. For fair comparison, we vary the data proportion under a fixed
training token budget. Specifically, introducing image editing supervision improves the average
understanding score from 62.64 to 64.49, while the OneIG average score decreases slightly from
0.538 to 0.528. These results suggest that cross-task transfer and interference can coexist within
a shared backbone, highlighting both the potential and challenges of joint optimization in highly
shared UMMs. Detailed results are provided in Appendix E.
4.6
DISCUSSION
The above findings motivate us to reflect on the broader goals of unified multimodal modeling. We
discuss three perspectives on its current challenges and future opportunities.
From coexistence to synergy.
We believe multimodal unification can be viewed at three levels.
Capability unification supports both tasks in one model. Collaborative unification allows under-
standing, reasoning, and generation to work together. Synergistic unification seeks mutual improve-
ment through shared knowledge and supervision. HIUNI mainly explores the first level, while CoT
image generation in Appendix D takes an initial step toward the second. The editing experiments in
Section 4.5 show potential cross-task benefits, but consistent synergy remains an open challenge.
Toward stronger cross-task transfer.
Beyond better training recipes, data and representation
alignment may play a more important role in positive transfer, particularly for fine-grained visual
capabilities. Recent studies on reconstruction alignment (Xie et al., 2026a), multi-representation
generation (Su et al., 2026), and capability transfer (Kang et al., 2026) support this direction. Struc-
tured data representations, such as XML-style captions or even image-related code, may connect
semantic understanding and visual generation more directly than conventional descriptions.
Native unification and agentic systems.
Agentic systems can manage long-term memory, plan-
ning, and tool use without native interleaved generation. Native UMMs, however, may better support
closely coupled understanding, reasoning, and generation within a shared context. We see these ap-
proaches as complementary, with agents handling long-term coordination and UMMs supporting
close multimodal interactions. Whether native interaction offers advantages over modular systems
remains an important open question.
5
CONCLUSION
We present HIUNI, a unified multimodal model that harmonizes visual understanding and generation
within a shared visual space. HIUNI reuses the language aligned ViT and dense Transformer of a
pretrained VLM for shared semantic modeling, while supplying generation specific spatial details
only near the output stage. Experiments show that strong understanding and generation can coexist
within such a highly shared architecture, without requiring separate visual encoders or task specific
backbones. Beyond HIUNI itself, we believe this simple and scalable design provides a promising
path to extend mature VLMs into more unified models with richer generative capabilities, broader
tasks, and more interactive multimodal scenarios.
